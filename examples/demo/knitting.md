Knitting is the process of interlooping a continuous length of yarn to create textile fabric made of rows of interconnected loops. Knitting can be done by hand, by machine or using one of a variety of frames developed for the purpose. When done by hand, one uses a pair of knitting needles where the loops are created one at a time. When done by machine the loops are created on hooks that work one entire row of knitting at a time.
Hand knitting needles are either straight with blunt ends or so-called "circular needles" that are a single flexible tube or wire with blunt needles at both ends. The resulting knitted fabric consists rows of loops that intermesh with the rows above and below.
Differences in yarn (varying in fiber type, yarn weight, color, uniformity, twist per inch), in needle size, and in stitch type allow for a variety of knitted fabrics with different properties, including texture, thickness, heat retention, and water resistance. A small sample of knitwork is known as a "swatch" and helps the maker determine the gauge of the intended work.


== Overview ==


=== Etymology of the words knit and knitting ===
There are several possible origins of the word knit and knitting. These include the German derivation from the word knutten (to tie), the Old Norse word knytja, and the Old English words cnyttan (to tie a knot, bind together, fasten by tieing) and cnotta (knot).


=== History and culture ===

Artifacts of fabric making using some form of knitting have been found in many regions including Egypt, China, Denmark, and Sweden, dating as far back as 4200 BCE. The exact origins of the invention of knitting are unknown, however the earliest known examples are cotton socks dating from the 11th century, found in the remains of the city of Fustat, now part of Cairo. In Europe, knitting is believed to be an evolution of the ancient craft of nålebinding, the Scandinavian word for "needle binding". In nålebinding, fabric is created through forming loops and knots of yarn with a single needle.
The first commercial knitting guilds appeared in Western Europe in the early fifteenth century (Tournai in 1429, Barcelona in 1496). The Guild of Saint Fiacre was founded in Paris in 1527, but the archives mention an organization (not necessarily a guild) of knitters from 1268. The occupation "cap knitter" was recorded in 1473 as these textile artists primarily produced knit caps and dyed stockings.
